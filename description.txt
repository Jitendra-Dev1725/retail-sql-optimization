This report presents a complete SQL query optimization and performance tuning study for a retail database built in PostgreSQL 16. I created six tables (stores, customers, products, inventory, orders and order_items) and generated realistic data: 300,000 orders, 900,000 order items, 100,000 inventory rows, 50,000 customers and 5,000 products. I then wrote seven sample queries that mimic typical retail operations such as monthly sales per store, customer order history, top-selling products, inventory lookups, yearly revenue, re-engagement lists and low-stock alerts, and recorded their baseline execution times using EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) with a warm-up run and the median of five runs.

The initial challenges were full table scans, non-sargable conditions (functions on date columns), SELECT *, a NOT IN subquery and one very large orders table. I fixed them step by step. First, I created single-column, composite and covering indexes. Second, I rewrote queries by removing SELECT *, replacing EXTRACT() filters with date ranges and converting NOT IN to NOT EXISTS. Third, I range-partitioned the orders table by year so that partition pruning skips unneeded data. Fourth, I created a materialized view to pre-aggregate product sales, because an index alone could not speed up the heavy top-products report. One extra index was tested, ignored by the planner and dropped.

The comparative metrics show total execution time falling by 54.7 percent, with the customer history lookup about 120 times faster, the low-stock alert 38 times faster and the inventory lookup 27 times faster. The report explains the rationale for every technique, includes before-and-after execution plans, charts, trade-offs such as index write overhead and materialized view freshness, and recommendations for further improvement such as pg_stat_statements, partial indexes, caching and read replicas. All SQL scripts, the benchmark script and raw results are available in the GitHub repository.
